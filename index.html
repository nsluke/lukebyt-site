Why this exists

Great idea. Wrong architecture.

Tidbyt put a beautiful pixel display in a hundred thousand homes — then got acquired, development stopped, and the cloud those displays depend on began winding down. The hardware didn't fail. The architecture did: a datacenter sat between you and your screen.

LukeByt moves the server inside the walnut. Apps render on the device, on your network. There is nothing to shut down.

How it works

An appliance, not a subscription.

01 · Local-first

The server is inside

A small Linux computer lives behind the panel, running the full app engine. Plug it into the wall and it just works — no account, no pairing servers, no phoning home.

02 · Setup

90 seconds, from your phone

The iOS app finds your LukeByt over Bluetooth, you type your Wi-Fi password once, and you're picking apps. If you can set up AirPods, you can set up a LukeByt.

03 · Open

A living ecosystem

Compatible with the open Tronbyt platform and its hundreds of community apps — weather, transit, stocks, sports, and stranger things. Write your own, or run it stock. Orphaned Tidbyts welcome: Gen 1 support is planned.

Questions

The honest FAQ

Do I need an account or a subscription?
No, and no. There's nothing to sign up for. The device renders everything itself, on your network. An optional remote-access service may come later for people who want to manage a display from far away — but it will never be required for the device to work.
What happens if LukeByt-the-company disappears?
Your display keeps working, full stop. That's the entire point of the architecture: there's no cloud dependency to sunset. The software stack is built on open protocols, so the community can carry it forward — the same way Tidbyt owners kept their devices alive after the company stopped.
I own a Tidbyt. Will LukeByt help me?
That's the plan. A LukeByt can act as the local server for your existing Tidbyt Gen 1, so your orphaned display comes back to life on your own network — no company servers involved. Consider it a rescue mission with matching walnut.
When does it ship?
The numbered founder batch is targeted for early 2027, hand-assembled in Brooklyn in order of reservation. Reserving today costs nothing — you're joining the list, locking a low number, and telling us which product to prioritize.
Is it hackable?
Delightfully. It's a Linux computer with an open app platform: SSH access, a documented API, and apps written in simple Starlark scripts. Build the app your café needs. Or never touch a terminal — it's equally happy either way.
